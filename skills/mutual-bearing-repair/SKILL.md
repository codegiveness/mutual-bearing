# Mutual Bearing Repair

Use when the collaboration itself needs diagnosis, not whenever a task contains ambiguity. If a correction already identifies the problem and repair, make that repair directly. This skill does not replace the task with an interview, authorize extra work, or require a visible diagnosis template.

## Locate the mismatch

Recover the relevant request, decisions, reported experience, available evidence, and actual work. Identify the earliest supported divergence that still affects the outcome. Distinguish what the user said from what you inferred, what was authorized from what was proposed, and what happened from what was merely claimed. Do not infer motives, personality, cognitive ability, or a medical condition from the interaction.

Use whichever distinctions change the next action; they overlap and are not a checklist:

- **Direction and meaning:** Are you solving the intended problem? An unresolved user-owned choice needs a question; an available answer needs recovery and use. An exploratory goal may need a concrete contrast, not a tighter specification.
- **Evidence and belief:** Is the interpretation relying on an unsupported fact or explanation? Accept the reported experience, investigate its cause, and keep unavailable evidence explicit. More agreement cannot supply a missing fact.
- **Pressures and boundaries:** Is behavior optimizing an easy proxy—brevity, visible progress, pleasing the user, defending prior work—instead of the agreed result? Correct the choice or success check, not a presumed motive. Being able to act is separate from being permitted to act.
- **Capability and conditions:** Is access, context, a tool, a skill, or a feasible method missing? Use available resources or identify the actual blocker. Do not keep rewriting a clear request to compensate for an unavailable capability.
- **Execution and coordination:** Was the agreement understood but implemented incorrectly, or did a correction fail to reach dependent work? Trace affected outputs and pending actions. For a failed handoff, check what context the recipient actually received or could access, not just what the sender recorded. An accurate recap without a changed artifact is not repair.

Treat causal explanations as hypotheses until supported. Several causes may coexist; do not blame the user or prescribe a remedy from a label alone.

## Choose the smallest adequate repair

Make the disputed interpretation and its consequence recognizable when they matter. Ask about the unresolved decision, with room to reject your framing, rather than presenting options that all preserve your premise. If context already resolves it, apply the answer. An already-authorized repair needs no new approval; a consequential new commitment still does. Keep independent work moving.

Repair the affected reasoning, artifact, queued action, or handoff, preserving unaffected decisions and permissions. If a completed action cannot be undone, state the outcome and available remedies honestly. Do not promise rollback or take a new consequential action without authority.

Check an observable consequence of the repair against the actual goal, not against a convenient proxy. If the remedy changes a rule, workflow, or control, check relevant side effects on authorized work and required safeguards rather than judging only the original symptom. Distinguish successful repair, incomplete work, missing evidence, and a remaining user decision. If repeated instructions have failed, consider context delivery, conflicting guidance, tool design, or an external permission control before adding more prose.

Return to the task once the mismatch is repaired or the precise blocker is exposed. Give only the explanation the user needs to understand what changed and influence what happens next; no compulsory postmortem, score, rewritten prompt, or ongoing repair mode.
